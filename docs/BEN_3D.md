# Ben’s volumetric character model

The “Ben in three dimensions” section contains a fully rounded Three.js character. His helmet, skull, ears, nose, torso, arms, jeans, shoes and Fizz Stunner have depth from every direction. The mesh uses one world unit per calibrated sprite pixel, with a standing ground plane at −0.5 and a physical helmet crown at 31.0. These are model units, not physical measurements.

The model is a posing and reference figure for the sprites, and a possible basis for a 3D game or print. It stays recognisably Ben, with the sprite’s colours and equipment, but uses more realistic proportions than the 2.9-head sprite:

| Measurement | Sprite-matched model | Current model |
|---|---|---|
| Heads tall (crown to chin) | 2.8 (chin at 19.9) | 3.6 (chin at 22.2) |
| Face width | 8.3, wider than the 6.9 torso | 6.0, narrower than the 8.0 helmet and 9.5 shirt shoulders |
| Ribcage / waist / hips (width × depth) | 6.9 × 5.1 / — / 6.4 | 4.9 × 3.9 / 4.5 × 3.6 / 5.1 × 3.7 |
| Shoulder joints / hip joints from the centre | 4.0 / 2.05 | 3.4 / 1.45 |
| Helmet crown above skull | 4.5 | 1.05 |
| Visible neck below the jaw | almost none | about 1.2 at the front, above a 0.9 collar band |
| Upper arm / forearm | 3.7 / 2.9 | 4.2 / 3.6 |
| Hand length (wrist to fingertip) | 1.75 | 2.6 |
| Thigh / shin | 3.3 / 3.5 | 4.2 / 3.9 |
| Shoe length / width | 8.7 / 3.6 (28% of height) | 6.1 / 2.5 (19%) |

Ben is a skinny kid: a narrow ribcage and shoulders, a thin waist and hips, slender legs and thin ankles, so the helmet and head read big on a small body. The arm muscles (deltoid, biceps and forearm) keep their earlier shape and size; the arms move inwards with the narrower shoulders, and the gap left under each arm keeps the sleeves free of the torso. The order of widths still follows the sprite: shirt shoulders, then helmet, then face. Tests in `src/model-3d/model.test.ts` hold these proportions.

## Builds: hybrid and current

Choose **Build** in the viewer controls. Two builds share the 26-bone rig, the pose and animation library in `poses.ts`, the turntable and the review tools:

- **Hybrid (artifact head, Codex body)** (`hybrid`, the default): `createHybridBenModel(svg, print)` in `src/model-3d/hybrid.ts`.
- **Current (PR #7)** (`current`): `createBenModel(svg, style)` in `src/model-3d/model.ts`, with head styles A–G.

The hybrid is composed from three sources. It builds the current model, keeps the parts that model does best, removes everything else by name and adds its own head and body:

The hybrid's neck, collar and shirt are built so they cannot open from any angle. The shirt is closed over the shoulders: a yoke ellipsoid covers the neck base and carries the back up behind it, and there is no neck hole. The neck is a smooth tube that starts deep inside the yoke, rises through the collar and flares into the jaw. The white collar is a rolled band with real thickness: one closed, smoothly shaded surface whose inner face lies inside the neck and whose skirt is traced 0.1–0.6 units below the yoke's surface at every angle. It is merged into the shirt mesh. Below the collar top the neck, collar and shirt are all skinned to the chest, so they move as one through the run twist, head turns and the whistling idle. No surfaces are coplanar, and no hole is cut through a smoothed mesh.

The Bubble trio, Fizz drop and Bottle cap are the artboard's chest logos, drawn as fine polygons, cut into short edges and laid in layers on the shirt's front surface, skinned with the shirt. The sprites show a yellow cross on the chest, which **Sprite cross** keeps. The Bubble trio uses the same yellow and brown, so it reads as the same Fizz print in a rounder shape.

`src/model-3d/hybrid.test.ts` checks the kept and replaced parts, finite normalised weights, disposal and print swaps, the head on its short neck, the skinny proportions, the Stunner side, the helmet fit all round (the skull stays inside the liner and within half a unit of it under the shell) and the belt standing off the body by one belt thickness at three heights. A sweep casts rays from 72 directions, from 55° below to 50° above, at points inside the neck and just under the neck skin below the collar top, in 14 poses including four phases of the whistling idle, run, climb, fall, jump, firing, phone and both looks. Each ray must meet an outside face first: a view into the inside of the shirt, neck or head, a view through to nothing or bare neck below the collar's top edge fails the test. Cameras inside the head, an arm or the shirt are skipped. A second test checks that the neck below y 20 stays inside the shirt and that the neck and collar wind outwards. The sleeve coverage test from the current model runs on the hybrid's arms.

`bun run model:review` writes the hybrid's plates to `models/ben-3d/review/hybrid/`: the same reference comparison, poses and action cycles as the current model, plus `hybrid-angles.png` (front, three-quarter, side, back and two from below, perspective) and `hybrid-neck-angles.png` (the same six views framed on the neck and collar). The hybrid's overall height is two pixels under the sprite at every angle, the edge of the tolerance, because the head and helmet are lowered one unit for the shorter neck.

Known limits: a fully raised arm (climb, jump) still creases the armpit fabric visibly, though no skin shows through it; the hybrid's head is not in the downloadable GLB, which still exports the current build.

## Head styles

With the current build, choose **Head style** in the viewer controls to swap Ben's head and face live; the pose and the rest of the model are kept. All seven styles share the cranium, so the same fitted helmet, hair line, neck and straps fit each one. Each style uses EGA palette colours, and its eyes are at most slightly lidded. `headStyles` in `src/model-3d/head.ts` defines them; `createBenModel(svg, style)` and `model.setHeadStyle(id)` select one. The default, and the style exported in the GLB, is A.

- **A · Soft cartoon** (`cartoon`): a round, soft head with big simple blue eyes, tiny highlights and a subtle lid, a small nose bump and a simple red smile.
- **B · Sprite-faithful** (`sprite`): the closest to the pixel sprite. A flat-fronted, faceted face with flat blue eye dots, the sprite's grey bumpy nose as a small block, blocky ears and grey banding under the chin. Its smile curves up at the corners, with a lighter upper-lip edge and a small crease at each corner.
- **C · Stylised** (`stylised`): halfway between the earlier sculpt and a cartoon. A fuller face with shallow sockets, lidded eyeballs, simple brow lines and light-red blushed cheeks.
- **D · Toy figure** (`toy`): a chunky toy-figure head. A glossy rounded cylinder with painted-on eyes and smile, a ball nose and nub ears.
- **E · Realistic (original)** (`realistic`): the first sculpted head, with a jaw line, brow ridge, carved eye sockets with lidded eyeballs, a bridged nose and lips. It sits on the same neck and collar as the other styles.
- **F · Sprite head, realer** (`sprite-real`): the sprite-faithful head made more real. It keeps the flat front, bumpy nose block, blue eye dots and big-headed proportions, and adds real ears, a narrower jaw with a gentle chin and defined cheekbones. It has the same smile as B.
- **G · Artifact kid** (`artifact`): the hybrid's artboard head on the current model's neck. Its egg face reaches lower, so the chin meets the longer neck; it has glossy painted blue eyes, a round nose, a U-shaped smile and small ears.

`bun run model:review` saves `head-style-cartoon.png`, `head-style-sprite.png`, `head-style-stylised.png`, `head-style-toy.png`, `head-style-realistic.png`, `head-style-sprite-real.png` and `head-style-artifact.png` in `models/ben-3d/review/`. Each shows the head, neck and collar at front, three-quarter and right-profile angles.

## Preview and rig

Run `bun run dev`, then choose **Rigged 3D** in the gallery navigation. Rotation runs independently of the sprite animations. Drag in Studio view to orbit, scroll to zoom, or use the angle slider. Choose Sprite orthographic view for a level comparison. Select a calibrated reference and press **Match reference view** to restore the standing pose at the reference angle; **Overlay reference** superimposes the original pixels.

The rig has 26 named bones: pelvis, spine, chest, neck, head, two upper arms, forearms, hands and thumbs, two thighs, shins, feet and toe joints, plus Stunner and phone attachments, two driven knee helpers and a muzzle-flash attachment. The shirt, shoulder caps, sleeves and collar share one smooth surface. Each sleeve is a tube around its upper-arm bone, hemmed square to the arm about 60% of the way to the elbow. Fabric closer to the sleeve than to the torso is skinned rigidly to the upper arm, and the shoulder cap blends from the chest above the joint into the arm below it, so the sleeve openings stay on the arms through raised, swinging and bent poses. The torso fabric blends from the pelvis at the hem through the spine and chest, so it follows the run's hip and shoulder twist. A small gap under each arm, and a seam blend limited to the shoulder, stop the sleeve and torso fusing. Each arm is one surface with a deltoid inside the sleeve, a biceps swell, a rounded elbow, a tapering forearm, a wrist, a palm, four jointed fingers and an opposing thumb. The hands are about 1.4 times the earlier size. The jeans connect a waistband, a rounded pelvis and seat, full thighs, knee caps and straight cuffs in one surface with blended joint weights. The thigh is longer than the shin and the hip joints sit inside the pelvis volume, ready for crossed-leg posing later. Fuller, straight cuffs continue into the shoe openings. Each driven knee helper takes half the shin rotation, keeping the knee cross-section round while the leg bends. Each running shoe is one connected surface, coloured as in the sprites: a red upper with a white rubber toe cap that wraps over the top of the shortened toe box, a white sole and a grey underside, with red laces and the yellow badge near the cuff. Its regions share seam vertices, and the forefoot blends into a toe joint for shoe flex. Face details, ears, helmet and hair follow the head bone; the neck blends into the head bone below the jaw. The ray-gun Stunner (`src/model-3d/stunner.ts`) has a purple body with a magenta charge bulb, a cyan charge window, a grey barrel with coils and three dark fins, a flared grey emitter dish with a small glowing tip, and a raked grip with dark panels and a trigger guard. It is holstered barrel-down on Ben’s anatomical right hip only, with the grip up and back. A dark holster band and belt strap stay on the hip when he draws it. The phone’s back faces the viewer and its screen faces Ben; dynamic screen lighting on his face remains a future lighting effect.

The pose selector provides standing, running, jumping, falling, climbing, firing, whistling, phone, looking up, looking down and belly-clutch poses. The 3D run samples the continuous sprite rig for waist bob, torso lean, shoulder twist, head turn and arm timing. The rig's torso turn drives a counter-rotation: the hips turn 7° one way and the shoulders 14° the other, bringing the right shoulder forward with the right arm. The neck takes back most of the shoulder turn so the head holds its line, then adds the rig's 5° camera-facing head turn. Its 0.75-unit bob amplitude and 0.04-cycle lag are shared directly with that rig, with canvas-down motion converted to world-up motion. A 0.55-unit crouch offset accommodates the 3D leg proportions. The 3D stride has a 2.6-unit half-length and a 1.9-unit recovery lift to avoid folding the foot into the hip. The shirt hem, belt, buckle and holster follow the pelvis together. Running uses the sprite rig’s 24% stance duration per foot, including flight between contacts, and two-bone leg inverse kinematics: a planted foot sweeps from front to back along the ground, then bends and recovers forwards through the air. Arms counter-swing against the legs, and heel strike and toe-off account for shoe pitch and forefoot flex. Run, Jump, Fall, Climb, Whistle, Firing, Look up and Look down animate continuously in the preview and exported GLB. The remaining entries are held rig poses.

- **Jump** (1.1 s): crouch with the arms back, launch with the arms swinging up and toes pushing off, tuck at the apex, reach the legs down and absorb the landing. Feet stay planted on the ground until lift-off and from touchdown.
- **Fall** (1.2 s): braced for impact rather than flailing. Ben is crouched slightly with his head tucked, his forearms drawn up in front of his chest and chin with palms out, and his knees soft with the feet lightly staggered. A gentle bob runs through the loop.
- **Climb** (1.4 s): a hand-over-hand ladder climb in place. Each hand holds a rung for half the cycle, descending with the ladder, then reaches two rungs up while the opposite foot steps. The hands grip wide, beside the face, and the head tips back so the helmet brim clears the rungs. The preview shows a ladder prop that scrolls with the climb; `ladder` and `ladderRungs` in `poses.ts` share its geometry.
- **Firing** (2.6 s): the right hand reaches to the hip and draws the Stunner, which follows the hand by its grip. Ben brings it up as he plants a split stance, turns his right shoulder towards the target and leans in. He locks the arm out at shoulder height with the left hand cupping the right, then fires with a cyan muzzle flash and a sharp upward recoil through gun, arm, chest and head. He recovers, lowers to low ready and reholsters. The `firing` phase table in `poses.ts` sets the timing. The look loops ease from neutral into a held gaze and back; looking down includes a gentle forward bow and outward arm balance. Their shared duration table drives both browser playback and clip export. These demonstrate the reusable model rig rather than replacing the existing six-frame sprite animations. Phone, firing and belly-clutch hands use two-bone inverse kinematics.

Open **Pose the rig and move Ben** to rotate individual joints or translate the whole character. Joint edits pause pose animation. **Save pose JSON** downloads the root position and orientation, plus every bone’s local position, quaternion and scale. This download does not overwrite sprite edits or the authored rig.

## Source and exports

- `source/ben-3d.json`: rest joints, calibrated dimensions and six sprite references.
- `source/ben-3d-skins.svg`: named surface artwork for the helmet stripe, chest emblem, shoe badge and phone mark.
- `src/model-3d/model.ts`: volumetric geometry, materials and skin bindings.
- `src/model-3d/head.ts`: the shared cranium, the head styles, the artboard face, the hairline and the hair cap built from the head surface.
- `src/model-3d/hybrid.ts`: the hybrid build: kept equipment from the current model, the artboard head, the Codex body, the closed neck and collar, and the chest prints.
- `src/model-3d/sculpt.ts`: smooth implicit surfaces, surface projection and continuous four-influence skinning. Sculpted surfaces keep their largest connected piece, then each vertex is snapped back onto the field and shaded with the field gradient, so curved parts do not show marching-cubes terracing. Cavity shading darkens sockets and creases towards a palette shade through vertex colours.
- `src/model-3d/equipment.ts`: the fitted, ventilated bicycle helmet, plus joined running-shoe geometry and surface profiles.
- `src/model-3d/rig.ts`: skeleton hierarchy and shared vertex skin weights.
- `src/model-3d/poses.ts`: skeletal poses, inverse kinematics and animation sampling.
- `src/model-3d/svg-skin.ts`: SVG parsing, tessellation, curved-surface projection and winding correction.
- `src/model-3d/preview.ts`: interactive Three.js preview and orthographic review renderer.
- `models/ben-3d/ben.glb`: portable skinned model containing ten named animation clips. Run, Jump, Fall, Climb, Whistle, Firing, Look up and Look down contain motion; the remaining clips hold their named poses. The ladder prop is preview-only and is not in the GLB. The phone attachment is hidden by scale outside the phone pose.
- `models/ben-3d/rig.json` and `skins.svg`: exported configuration and surface artwork.

Run `bun run model:build` to regenerate the GLB, rig description and skin artwork. Run `bun run generate` after preview changes to refresh the standalone HTML. `bun run build` packages the model and documentation with the app. The standalone HTML embeds the procedural model and Three.js; downloadable companion files require the surrounding project folder.

## Proportion review

Run a development server, then `bun run model:review`. Set `MODEL_REVIEW_URL` to review another served build. The command uses Playwright and system Chromium at `/usr/bin/chromium`; set `CHROMIUM_PATH` for another installation. Software WebGL is enabled for reproducible headless rendering. Run `bun run model:build` separately to refresh the downloadable GLB after a source change.

The review renders front, right quarter, right profile, rear, left profile and left quarter views with a fixed 32 × 44 orthographic projection. It compares occupied height, helmet width, visible head-band width, torso-band width, combined shoe width and shoe height against the saved calibrated reference sprites. Height and helmet width use a two-pixel tolerance. The face, the skinny-kid torso and the shoes are deliberately smaller than the sprite, so `reviewChecks` in `source/ben-3d.json` widens only those ranges, with the reason beside each. Head width may be up to four pixels narrower, the shirt-and-arms band up to four pixels narrower (most at the quarters), combined shoe width up to five pixels narrower (mostly in profile) and shoe height up to three pixels lower. `metrics.json` records the ranges used. The source helmet occupies rows 2–8, the head comparison uses rows 9–13, the torso and arms use rows 15–23, and shoes occupy rows 30–34. Helmet, clothing and shoe references exclude black outline pixels: that border defines the pixel drawing, not the physical thickness of the 3D shell or shirt. Head width is rendered separately from the helmet so a lower brim does not inflate the head measurement. Overall height retains the complete sprite bounds for alignment; shoe dimensions follow the coloured upper and sole. Bounds count rendered pixels at alpha ≥ 128. The report exits unsuccessfully for a dimension outside tolerance, browser exceptions or horizontal mobile overflow.

`models/ben-3d/review/reference-comparison.png` places the six model renders above the source sprites in angle order 0°, 45°, 90°, 180°, 270°, 315°. `poses.png` shows Standing, Run, Jump, Fall, Climb, Firing, Whistle, Phone, Look up, Look down and Belly clutch, read left to right and top to bottom. Pose framing allows extra headroom for jumping. `run-cycle.png` shows six chronological right-profile running phases at 0, 1/6, 2/6, 3/6, 4/6 and 5/6 of a cycle, with Ben facing right. `firing-cycle.png`, `jump-cycle.png`, `fall-cycle.png`, `climb-cycle.png`, `look-up-cycle.png` and `look-down-cycle.png` show six timed samples of each action. `metrics.json` records the reference-mask choices, measurements and browser checks. `run-body-motion.json`, written by `bun run model:build`, compares the source and 3D waist displacement, torso pitch, knee flexion, ankle-target reach and foot support state at 61 phases. Tests also verify that the shirt hem stays fixed to the pelvis throughout the run.

The green bicycle helmet fits the skull. Its inner liner sits 0.12 units outside the hair and its outer shell crowns about one unit above the skull, so it reads as a helmet rather than a cap on hair. The shell is a sculpted solid with foam thickness: four roof channels on each side of a solid central rib, interrupted by a bridge across the crown, plus a side vent above each temple, all with rounded edges. The lower edge sits just above the brow, rises over the ears and drops over the occiput. A grey trim band runs around the lower shell and stands proud as a short rounded lip at the front, matching the sprite’s grey rim without becoming a visor. The white stripe follows the shell along the central rib. Webbing straps 0.15 units in radius run in front of and behind each ear to a grey divider, then under the jaw to a chin buckle. Close-cropped brown-blond hair shows at the temples and nape below the shell.

The head is one sculpted surface per style: the shared cranium joined to that style's face, with the features described under [Head styles](#head-styles). The white at the neck is the shirt’s collar: a turned cotton band about 0.9 units tall, joined into the shirt surface and coloured white there, around an open neckline. A short, sturdy skin-coloured neck rises from it, about 3.2 units wide at the collar. It leans back as it rises, so the jaw rests on its front and the base of the skull, carried down by a nape in every head style, rests on its back. The collar wraps the neck base and climbs at the back, where it sits on the shoulders; the helmet straps lie along the jaw. The collar's white edge follows a smooth function of position, so it meets the red shirt in a clean line.

The shirt has a narrow chest, shoulder slopes and short sleeves, and fills out at the waist where it tucks into slim jeans. The belt is a leather band traced around the jeans waistband at each angle, so it wraps the body with no gap instead of floating as a loop. The holster hangs from it by a short strap on the right hip. Red running shoes use a low, thinly bound ankle opening, a shortened heel tucked closer to the ankle, a narrower forefoot, lower vamp and toe box, thinner curved white sole with a grey underside, a white toe cap, red laces and yellow side badges centred beneath the trouser cuffs. The rear quarter is lower and narrower so it does not form a padded projection behind the leg. Upper and sole meet along shared vertices in a single mesh, so they cannot drift apart under toe flex. Ground-contact sampling uses the same shoe profile and toe pivot as the geometry.

The construction review used [Giro’s Isode Mips II photographs](https://www.giro.com/product/isode-mips-ii/100000000200000184.html), [Under Armour’s red Pursuit running-shoe photographs](https://underarmour.co.nz/products/mens-charged-pursuit-4-bl-redredcardinal) and [ASICS’ running-shoe anatomy](https://www.asics.com/au/en-au/blog/the-anatomy-of-a-running-shoe/) and [Nike Free RN heel photographs](https://www.nike.com/jp/en/t/free-rn-nn-mens-road-running-shoes-QtDLmK), alongside the original standing sprites. The sprite files remain the authored reference artwork.

This is a proportional interpretation of pixel references. It preserves a single body and shoe volume through rotation, while the source sprites contain hand-drawn outlines, different arm positions, staggered feet and angle-specific exaggeration. A matching bounding box does not establish an identical silhouette or a finished likeness. Visual review remains necessary for facial expression, surface treatment, joint deformation and unseen rear surfaces. The existing 137 sprite frames remain unchanged by model building and review.

## SVG surface artwork and Jar

[Jar’s model helpers](https://github.com/liminal-hq/jar/tree/527c5c34f62a8467e133249615a19fe7ca45040e/apps/jar/src/render/models) use named SVG paths, bevelled extrusions, decals, pivots and vertex deformation for fish and snails. The useful ideas here are named artwork layers, explicit authored coordinates and details that follow their owner’s movement. Ben’s body uses smoothly joined sculpted surfaces and curved meshes; SVG layers decorate those surfaces. No Jar implementation or SVG asset was copied.

The helmet stripe is tessellated before mapping onto the rounded cap. Triangle winding is corrected after projecting SVG’s downward Y axis into model space. Shoe badges project onto the outer ankle panels and follow the foot bones; the chest emblem follows the chest. Base material colours come from the game palette; skin stays the palette’s white. Each material has its own finish: a clear-coated glossy shell, matte foam, cotton and denim with soft sheen, slightly glossy skin and lips, wet-looking eyes, rubber soles, a metal buckle and plastic for the Stunner and phone. Studio lighting and subtle cavity shading produce the shades between palette entries. The studio key light uses a larger shadow bias to avoid self-shadow stripes on the smooth surfaces.

Three.js references: [OrthographicCamera](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/OrthographicCamera.html), [GLTFExporter](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFExporter.html) and [WebGLRenderer](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGLRenderer.html).

## Motion still to build

The lab's sprite rigs also cover the ledge pull, pogo, death and phone/sitting performances, which have no 3D counterpart yet. Pogo needs a 3D pogo stick, death a comic wobble, and phone a cross-legged seated pose. Whistle is still a held head tilt rather than the planned sway, foot tap and hands on the belt. The climb's fingers stay straight on the rungs instead of curling around them. The fall is built from the brace direction given for the sprite fall rig, so it should be re-matched once that rig is published. The face has no expressions, so the fall's determined look is carried by the pose alone. One head style is to be chosen and refined.

## Coverage and clean edges

The shirt, hair and collar are built so skin cannot show through them in any pose.

- **Sleeves:** each sleeve is skinned rigidly to its upper arm below the shoulder. The shirt's shoulder cap and the arm skin beneath it share one chest-to-arm blend, so the deltoid cannot rise through the fabric. The shirt draws a fraction in front of skin it lies against, so contact never flickers.
- **Collar and neck:** a yoke of shirt fabric wraps the neck base from the upper back up under the collar band, and the upper back is deep enough to sit behind the neck, so the nape is covered below the collar as well as inside it. The yoke narrows as it descends, so it forms no shelf seen from below. The collar band wraps a fixed neckline, and the neck is slightly wider than the band's inner face, so they overlap rather than meet edge to edge. The band carries 0.7 units down into the shirt, so no slit opens beneath it even from below. The upper back stops just under the band, which sits on it rather than inside it. The shirt's neck opening starts just inside the band at each angle, so the shirt back stays whole below the collar. The neck is skinned to the chest up to above the collar and narrows inside the shoulders below it, so it stays seated through the run twist and head turns. Rolled edges finish the collar's top and bottom.
- **Hair:** the cap keeps every head triangle that reaches the hair region, so it has no holes. Its thickness and colour fade into the skin across the hairline.
- **Edges:** rolled hems finish the sleeve cuffs, the helmet is lined with grey foam, and the chest emblem draws in front of the shirt, so it stays crisp from below and up close.

A test casts rays from outside in eight poses (standing, two run phases, climb, fall, jump, firing and phone). It aims at sleeve-covered arm skin (from straight out and from below), the scalp, and 64 points round the neck at four heights from below the collar to its top, swept from 75° below to 25° above. Fabric, its rolled edges or hair must be met first. The review API's `captureView(eye, look, pose, phase)` renders a perspective view from any camera position for close inspection from below, behind or the sides.

## Hands and the phone

Each hand has a thumb bone at the thumb's base, so the thumb can swing independently of the fingers. In the phone pose the phone tilts back towards Ben's eyes. The palms and fingers hold its back, and each thumb swings round the side edge onto the screen. A test checks that no part of either hand or forearm passes through the phone body. `phoneHold` in `poses.ts` sets the grip.
