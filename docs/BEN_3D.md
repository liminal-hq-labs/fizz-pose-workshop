# Ben’s volumetric character model

The “Ben in three dimensions” section contains a fully rounded Three.js character. His helmet, skull, ears, nose, torso, arms, jeans, shoes and Fizz Stunner have depth from every direction. The mesh uses one world unit per calibrated sprite pixel, with a standing ground plane at −0.5 and a physical helmet crown at 31.0. These are model units, not physical measurements.

The model is a posing and reference figure for the sprites, and a possible basis for a 3D game or print. It stays recognisably Ben, with the sprite’s colours and equipment, but uses more realistic proportions than the 2.9-head sprite:

| Measurement | Sprite-matched model | Current model |
|---|---|---|
| Heads tall (crown to chin) | 2.8 (chin at 19.9) | 3.4 (chin at 21.85) |
| Face width | 8.3, wider than the 6.9 torso | 6.0, narrower than the 7.0 torso and 8.0 helmet |
| Helmet crown above skull | 4.5 | 1.05 |
| Visible neck below the jaw | almost none | about 1.2 at the front, above a 0.9 collar band |
| Upper arm / forearm | 3.7 / 2.9 | 4.2 / 3.6 |
| Hand length (wrist to fingertip) | 1.75 | 2.6 |
| Thigh / shin | 3.3 / 3.5 | 4.2 / 3.9 |
| Shoe length | 8.7 (28% of height) | 6.8 (22%) |

The order of widths follows the sprite: shirt shoulders, then helmet, then face. Tests in `src/model-3d/model.test.ts` hold these proportions.

## Preview and rig

Run `bun run dev`, then choose **Rigged 3D** in the gallery navigation. Rotation runs independently of the sprite animations. Drag in Studio view to orbit, scroll to zoom, or use the angle slider. Choose Sprite orthographic view for a level comparison. Select a calibrated reference and press **Match reference view** to restore the standing pose at the reference angle; **Overlay reference** superimposes the original pixels.

The rig has 24 named bones: pelvis, spine, chest, neck, head, two upper arms, forearms and hands, two thighs, shins, feet and toe joints, plus Stunner and phone attachments, two driven knee helpers and a muzzle-flash attachment. The shirt and shoulders share one smooth surface, with weights blending from the pelvis at the hem through the spine, chest and upper arms. Each arm is one surface with a deltoid inside the sleeve, a biceps swell, a rounded elbow, a tapering forearm, a wrist, a palm, four jointed fingers and an opposing thumb. The hands are about 1.4 times the earlier size. The jeans connect a waistband, a rounded pelvis and seat, full thighs, knee caps and straight cuffs in one surface with blended joint weights. The thigh is longer than the shin and the hip joints sit inside the pelvis volume, ready for crossed-leg posing later. Fuller, straight cuffs continue into the shoe openings. Each driven knee helper takes half the shin rotation, keeping the knee cross-section round while the leg bends. Each running shoe is one connected surface: its red upper and white sole share seam vertices, and the forefoot blends into a toe joint for shoe flex. Face details, ears, helmet and hair follow the head bone; the neck blends into the head bone below the jaw. The Stunner remains on Ben’s anatomical right hip when standing and moves into his hands for the firing pose. The phone’s back faces the viewer and its screen faces Ben; dynamic screen lighting on his face remains a future lighting effect.

The pose selector provides standing, continuous running, jumping, climbing, firing, whistling, phone, looking up, looking down and belly-clutch poses. The 3D run samples the continuous sprite rig for waist bob, torso lean, shoulder twist and arm timing. Its 0.75-unit bob amplitude and 0.04-cycle lag are shared directly with that rig, with canvas-down motion converted to world-up motion. A 0.55-unit crouch offset accommodates the 3D leg proportions. The 3D stride has a 2.6-unit half-length and a 1.9-unit recovery lift to avoid folding the foot into the hip. The shirt hem, belt, buckle and holster follow the pelvis together. Running uses the sprite rig’s 24% stance duration per foot, including flight between contacts, and two-bone leg inverse kinematics: a planted foot sweeps from front to back along the ground, then bends and recovers forwards through the air. Arms counter-swing against the legs, and heel strike and toe-off account for shoe pitch and forefoot flex. Run, Whistle, Firing, Look up and Look down animate continuously in the preview and exported GLB. The remaining entries are held rig poses. Firing raises the Stunner, aims, recoils with a cyan muzzle flash and returns to low ready. The look loops ease from neutral into a held gaze and back; looking down includes a gentle forward bow and outward arm balance. Their shared duration table drives both browser playback and clip export. These demonstrate the reusable model rig rather than replacing the existing six-frame sprite animations. Phone, firing and belly-clutch hands use two-bone inverse kinematics.

Open **Pose the rig and move Ben** to rotate individual joints or translate the whole character. Joint edits pause pose animation. **Save pose JSON** downloads the root position and orientation, plus every bone’s local position, quaternion and scale. This download does not overwrite sprite edits or the authored rig.

## Source and exports

- `source/ben-3d.json`: rest joints, calibrated dimensions and six sprite references.
- `source/ben-3d-skins.svg`: named surface artwork for the helmet stripe, chest emblem, shoe badge and phone mark.
- `src/model-3d/model.ts`: volumetric geometry, materials and skin bindings.
- `src/model-3d/head.ts`: the sculpted head, face and eye placement, and the hair cap built from the head surface.
- `src/model-3d/sculpt.ts`: smooth implicit surfaces, surface projection and continuous four-influence skinning. Sculpted surfaces keep their largest connected piece, then each vertex is snapped back onto the field and shaded with the field gradient, so curved parts do not show marching-cubes terracing. Cavity shading darkens sockets and creases towards a palette shade through vertex colours.
- `src/model-3d/equipment.ts`: the fitted, ventilated bicycle helmet, plus joined running-shoe geometry and surface profiles.
- `src/model-3d/rig.ts`: skeleton hierarchy and shared vertex skin weights.
- `src/model-3d/poses.ts`: skeletal poses, inverse kinematics and animation sampling.
- `src/model-3d/svg-skin.ts`: SVG parsing, tessellation, curved-surface projection and winding correction.
- `src/model-3d/preview.ts`: interactive Three.js preview and orthographic review renderer.
- `models/ben-3d/ben.glb`: portable skinned model containing nine named animation clips. Run, Whistle, Firing, Look up and Look down contain motion; the remaining clips hold their named poses. The phone attachment is hidden by scale outside the phone pose.
- `models/ben-3d/rig.json` and `skins.svg`: exported configuration and surface artwork.

Run `bun run model:build` to regenerate the GLB, rig description and skin artwork. Run `bun run generate` after preview changes to refresh the standalone HTML. `bun run build` packages the model and documentation with the app. The standalone HTML embeds the procedural model and Three.js; downloadable companion files require the surrounding project folder.

## Proportion review

Run a development server, then `bun run model:review`. Set `MODEL_REVIEW_URL` to review another served build. The command uses Playwright and system Chromium at `/usr/bin/chromium`; set `CHROMIUM_PATH` for another installation. Software WebGL is enabled for reproducible headless rendering. Run `bun run model:build` separately to refresh the downloadable GLB after a source change.

The review renders front, right quarter, right profile, rear, left profile and left quarter views with a fixed 32 × 44 orthographic projection. It compares occupied height, helmet width, visible head-band width, torso-band width, combined shoe width and shoe height against the saved calibrated reference sprites. Height, helmet width and torso width use a two-pixel tolerance. The face and shoes are deliberately smaller than the sprite, so `reviewChecks` in `source/ben-3d.json` widens only those ranges, with the reason beside each: head width may be up to four pixels narrower, combined shoe width up to five pixels narrower (mostly in profile) and shoe height up to three pixels lower. `metrics.json` records the ranges used. The source helmet occupies rows 2–8, the head comparison uses rows 9–13, the torso and arms use rows 15–23, and shoes occupy rows 30–34. Helmet, clothing and shoe references exclude black outline pixels: that border defines the pixel drawing, not the physical thickness of the 3D shell or shirt. Head width is rendered separately from the helmet so a lower brim does not inflate the head measurement. Overall height retains the complete sprite bounds for alignment; shoe dimensions follow the coloured upper and sole. Bounds count rendered pixels at alpha ≥ 128. The report exits unsuccessfully for a dimension outside tolerance, browser exceptions or horizontal mobile overflow.

`models/ben-3d/review/reference-comparison.png` places the six model renders above the source sprites in angle order 0°, 45°, 90°, 180°, 270°, 315°. `poses.png` shows Standing, Run, Jump, Climb, Firing, Whistle, Phone, Look up, Look down and Belly clutch, read left to right and top to bottom. Pose framing allows extra headroom for jumping. `run-cycle.png` shows six chronological right-profile running phases at 0, 1/6, 2/6, 3/6, 4/6 and 5/6 of a cycle, with Ben facing right. `firing-cycle.png`, `look-up-cycle.png` and `look-down-cycle.png` show six timed samples of each action. `metrics.json` records the reference-mask choices, measurements and browser checks. `run-body-motion.json`, written by `bun run model:build`, compares the source and 3D waist displacement, torso pitch, knee flexion, ankle-target reach and foot support state at 61 phases. Tests also verify that the shirt hem stays fixed to the pelvis throughout the run.

The green bicycle helmet fits the skull. Its inner liner sits 0.12 units outside the hair and its outer shell crowns about one unit above the skull, so it reads as a helmet rather than a cap on hair. The shell is a sculpted solid with foam thickness: four roof channels on each side of a solid central rib, interrupted by a bridge across the crown, plus a side vent above each temple, all with rounded edges. The lower edge sits just above the brow, rises over the ears and drops over the occiput. A grey trim band runs around the lower shell and stands proud as a short rounded lip at the front, matching the sprite’s grey rim without becoming a visor. The white stripe follows the shell along the central rib. Webbing straps 0.15 units in radius run in front of and behind each ear to a grey divider, then under the jaw to a chin buckle. Close-cropped brown-blond hair shows at the temples and nape below the shell.

The head is one sculpted surface: a rounded cranium, cheekbones, a brow ridge, carved eye sockets, a bridged nose with nostril wings, a forward upper jaw and lips, a jaw line and a chin. Eyeballs sit in the sockets with blue irises, pupils and thin lids. The white at the neck is the shirt’s collar: a turned cotton band about 0.9 units tall, joined into the shirt surface and coloured white there, around an open neckline. A skin-coloured neck rises from it.

The shirt has a chest, a narrower waist tucked into the jeans, shoulder slopes, deltoids and longer short sleeves. Red running shoes use a low, thinly bound ankle opening, a shortened heel tucked closer to the ankle, a narrower forefoot, lower vamp and toe box, thinner curved white sole, white laces and yellow side badges centred beneath the trouser cuffs. The rear quarter is lower and narrower so it does not form a padded projection behind the leg. Upper and sole meet along shared vertices in a single mesh, so they cannot drift apart under toe flex. Ground-contact sampling uses the same shoe profile and toe pivot as the geometry.

The construction review used [Giro’s Isode Mips II photographs](https://www.giro.com/product/isode-mips-ii/100000000200000184.html), [Under Armour’s red Pursuit running-shoe photographs](https://underarmour.co.nz/products/mens-charged-pursuit-4-bl-redredcardinal) and [ASICS’ running-shoe anatomy](https://www.asics.com/au/en-au/blog/the-anatomy-of-a-running-shoe/) and [Nike Free RN heel photographs](https://www.nike.com/jp/en/t/free-rn-nn-mens-road-running-shoes-QtDLmK), alongside the original standing sprites. The sprite files remain the authored reference artwork.

This is a proportional interpretation of pixel references. It preserves a single body and shoe volume through rotation, while the source sprites contain hand-drawn outlines, different arm positions, staggered feet and angle-specific exaggeration. A matching bounding box does not establish an identical silhouette or a finished likeness. Visual review remains necessary for facial expression, surface treatment, joint deformation and unseen rear surfaces. The existing 137 sprite frames remain unchanged by model building and review.

## SVG surface artwork and Jar

[Jar’s model helpers](https://github.com/liminal-hq/jar/tree/527c5c34f62a8467e133249615a19fe7ca45040e/apps/jar/src/render/models) use named SVG paths, bevelled extrusions, decals, pivots and vertex deformation for fish and snails. The useful ideas here are named artwork layers, explicit authored coordinates and details that follow their owner’s movement. Ben’s body uses smoothly joined sculpted surfaces and curved meshes; SVG layers decorate those surfaces. No Jar implementation or SVG asset was copied.

The helmet stripe is tessellated before mapping onto the rounded cap. Triangle winding is corrected after projecting SVG’s downward Y axis into model space. Shoe badges project onto the outer ankle panels and follow the foot bones; the chest emblem follows the chest. Base material colours come from the game palette; skin stays the palette’s white. Each material has its own finish: a clear-coated glossy shell, matte foam, cotton and denim with soft sheen, slightly glossy skin and lips, wet-looking eyes, rubber soles, a metal buckle and plastic for the Stunner and phone. Studio lighting and subtle cavity shading produce the shades between palette entries. The studio key light uses a larger shadow bias to avoid self-shadow stripes on the smooth surfaces.

Three.js references: [OrthographicCamera](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/OrthographicCamera.html), [GLTFExporter](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFExporter.html) and [WebGLRenderer](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGLRenderer.html).
