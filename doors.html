Keyed doors

A keyed door is a column of 16 × 16 terrain segments standing on the floor line: the top segment carries the badge (white triangle on red, white disc on blue), the bottom one the lock, and middles repeat. Two tiles stack top and bottom; one tile is the top alone. The key opens the door on the tick it touches it; the opening frames are presentation only, drawn before Ben, so Ben is always in front of the whole door. Each card shows the opening with Ben waiting beside the door, then Ben running across the open doorway (walk-run-6). Red and blue share every grid and differ only in colour and badge shape. Three tiles (16 × 48) is the standard height; two tiles is shown as a minimum only. A held open door keeps its lintel strip and side faces as decoration, never blocking Ben.

Exits

The exit is a 20 × 35 doorway on the far plane: its sill sits on the far edge of the floor, higher on screen than the floor line Ben stands on in front of it. It is placed 4 px right of its cell and 35 px above the top of the lower exit cell, with the 14 × 32 light on its own glow layer. Ben presses Up while overlapping it; the walk-in uses ben_doorIn1 to ben_doorIn8 placed by source/ben-door-pass.json, and the front lip (the bottom 6 rows) is drawn over him. In Fizz 2 the floor line is 16 px, one tile, below the sill, so standing Ben's head is 17 px above the sill inside the 35 px arch; the control above can still try other distances.

    Layer contract

      • back: the whole keyed door; for exits, the arch, jambs and any leaf or shutter (20 × 29, everything above the lip).
      • glow: the badge or the exit's light, drawn in the glow bank so the night pass leaves it at full brightness. Back cells under a glow cell are transparent, and an exit's glow frame lights only what its back frame leaves open.
      • front (exits only): the lip, the bottom 6 rows above the sill across the full 20 px. Drawn in the ground pass while Ben plays, and again after Ben during the walk-in. A step's glowOver redraws light between Ben and the lip.
      • Keyed doors have no front layer: they are terrain, drawn before Ben, and Ben overlaps one only once it is open.
      • Costs count 4 bits a pixel, ceil(w × h / 2) bytes a frame, once for each distinct grid.
